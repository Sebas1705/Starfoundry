# 0031. Construction rules: machines and belts are built on the floor of a fabrication bay; the starter ship has a workbench, not a factory; log version 13

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Area: simulation, content, gameplay, client

## Context
Since [ADR 0018](0018-modular-structures.md) a ship or station is a grid of 8 by 4 by 8 cell modules, and a machine or a belt may stand in any cell of any module whose kind lists its role (`allows` in the content): the `core` and the `workshop` take machines and belts, the `hull` takes belts, the `defence` module takes turrets and walls. Three things came out of play-testing and of the owner's review:

- **Free 3D placement.** The build tool plays on a horizontal plane that the player moves up and down with `Z` and `X` (four layers per module). Machines and belts float at any height of the volume, belts cross over each other on different layers, and nothing says where a floor is. The pre-built layouts (the starter ship, the bot) use layers 1, 2 and 3 of the workshop to stack lines.
- **Belts cannot be demolished by hand.** The simulation has always had `RemoveSegment` (it removes a whole belt line and refunds its cost, as `RemoveMachine` does for a machine). The fault is in the client: a machine is found by a 3D ray against its cell box, but a belt was only found through the build plane at the current layer, so a belt on another layer, or one the crosshair crossed above or below the plane, was never the thing aimed at, and the right click did nothing.
- **The ship starts as a finished factory.** The starter ship carries a workshop with an ore port, a smelter, an assembler and a depot already built. Nothing in the first minutes asks the player to build anything, and no module is "the place where things are built" as a decision.

The owner's direction: building inside a ship is only possible in certain specific modules; belts must be demolishable; constructions are at floor level in the module, not free in the volume; the initial ship has no buildable module but a basic fabrication table and an inventory; ships and stations are fully modular and customisable.

Constraints from earlier decisions: the simulation is deterministic and integer only ([ADR 0002](0002-deterministic-simulation-core.md)); every change goes through a logged command ([ADR 0003](0003-commands-as-the-only-way-to-change-the-world.md)); old command logs replay as recorded behind a rule flag and a log version, and the golden checksums of the existing worlds do not move ([ADR 0027](0027-mining-laser-rules.md), [ADR 0028](0028-world-generation-obstacles-and-collisions.md)); the scripted player (`tools/Starfoundry.Balance`, the structure bot) must still win the campaign on Normal.

## Decision
- **The floor.** In every module the cells at local layer `my * 4` (`StationGrid.FloorLayer` = 0 of the module, `StationGrid.IsFloorLayer`) are its floor: the 8 by 8 cells that rest on the deck. A machine or a belt cell may only stand on a floor cell; any other layer is refused with the new reject `NotOnFloor`. A belt is a straight line of floor cells in one of the four horizontal directions, so a belt in a vertical direction is refused too (its second cell is above the floor), and belts do not cross: a crossing needs a different layer. Walls and turrets obey the same rule (they stand on the floor of a defence module).
- **Which modules can be built on (`buildable`).** A module kind has a content flag `buildable` (`ModuleKindDef.BuildableFloor`). It defaults to "the kind allows something" so content without the flag behaves as before. In the shipped content:
  - `workshop` is renamed **Fabrication bay** (es: *Bahía de fabricación*; the content id stays `workshop` and so do saves and the enum): machines of every role but turrets and walls, and belts. Its cost rises from 24 ingots to 24 ingots and 8 plates, so adding one is a decision.
  - `core` (a station's heart) stays buildable, like a bay: a station is where structures are founded and its core floor is its first bay.
  - `defence` is buildable for turrets and walls, and now also belts (a belt may carry ammo over the seam into the defence module). Nothing else.
  - `hull` stays a belt-less connector (`buildable` false in the content although it lists `belt`), and cockpit, engine, reactor, cargo, drone bay, shield, sensor, Mk2 reactor and dock never allowed anything.
  A module of a kind that is not buildable refuses with the existing `KindNotAllowed`; the text now names the modules that do allow it.
- **Belts between bays.** A belt may run from one buildable module into the next of the same level, because every cell it crosses is a floor cell of a buildable module. A belt cannot go through a hull, cargo or other module. A bay beside a defence module is how ammo reaches turrets; a defence module on top of a bay no longer is, since its floor is a layer the bay's belts never reach.
- **Starter ship: a workbench and a hold, no factory.** The starter ship is cockpit, hull, cargo, reactor and engine (the second module was a workshop). It has no machine, no belt and no buildable module. It still has its **workbench**: the ship's crafting table, which already existed as the micro-refinery (`SetShipRecipe`, key `F`, a ship-role machine that is not placed in a structure and works from the hold). The workbench now has three recipes, all slow (twice the time of a machine, as before): iron ore to ingot (`ship_smelt`, 4 s), two ingots to a plate (`ship_press`, 4 s) and an ingot to four rounds of ammo (`ship_ammo`, 2 s). `F` cycles through them. The hold is the inventory.
- **Becoming a factory.** A player adds a fabrication bay to the ship with the module tool (`N`) and builds the factory on its floor, or founds a station (`I`) and builds on its core floor, or both. The bay needs 24 ingots and 8 plates, all of which the workbench can make from hand-mined ore, so the first minutes are: mine, make ingots and plates at the workbench, add a bay, build the ore port, smelter, assembler and depot, and the factory takes over. No research gate: the campaign stays winnable and the guided steps stay short.
- **Demolishing.** No new command. `RemoveMachine` and `RemoveSegment` stay as they were (a machine refunds its full cost, a belt line the cost of every cell, in campaign worlds and within build range; modules refund half). The client now finds belts the way it finds machines: a ray against each belt segment's box, so right click demolishes the belt or machine under the crosshair at any layer and in any direction, and a belt in range under the crosshair gets its own context card.
- **The client builds on the floor.** With the new rule, the build tool no longer has layers: the ghost snaps to the floor cells of the bay under the crosshair (the first buildable floor the ray crosses), `Z` and `X` no longer move a plane (they still choose the module row for the module tool), the cell helper is drawn on the floor of the bay under the cursor only, and a module that is not buildable shows its reason in red. A world that carries `LegacyBuilding` plays as before, with layers.
- **Compatibility.**
  - The command log version is 13. It adds no command and no tag (`43` to `45` stay free): it only gates the rule. Logs of versions 5 to 12 read with `WorldRules.LegacyBuilding` on (bit 4 of the world rules flags byte, the free bit `ADR 0030` left), so they replay with free building as recorded. Every new world leaves it off. The flag is not a difficulty setting and not in the presets or the new-game screen.
  - Like the other legacy flags it is hashed away: a world whose only non-default rule is a legacy flag hashes as default rules, so the golden checksums of the existing worlds hold when those worlds carry `LegacyBuilding`.
  - `content` is hashed with the new `buildable` flag only when it differs from its default, so content that does not use it (the stripped content of the golden tests) hashes as before. The shipped content changes (hull, workshop cost, the starter template, new recipes, recipes appended at the end so indices do not move) and so its hash, which, as with every content change since ADR 0024, means a save made with older content refuses to load; the command log versions and flags are for replays against matching content.
  - **Content that predates the rule.** The content root may carry `"floor_building": false` (absent means true): worlds made with it build freely, as with `LegacyBuilding`, and its templates are validated against the old rule. The flag is not mixed into the content hash (so the golden checksums of the stripped test content hold). The shipped content never sets it; `tests/Starfoundry.Sim.Tests/legacy/core_pre0031.json` is the content as it was before this change with the flag set, and the older structure tests run against it.
  - Templates are validated for the rule when the content loads (a machine or belt not on a floor cell of a module that allows it is a content error), so a scenario cannot start with an illegal layout.
- **Scenarios.** Levels 2 and 3 keep a fabrication bay on their pre-built ships and stations (they are later levels: their briefings give the player a working factory), on the floor now; the siege station puts its defence module beside the bay and runs the ammo belts into it. The first level starts from the starter ship: its goals (mine 60 ore, make 10 plates, found a station) are met with the workbench and its briefing and story beats say so. The sandbox and custom starts use the starter ship (free building, so a bay costs nothing there).
- **Not decided here:** building on the floor of an upside-down or turned ship (the floor is the module's own deck; the ghost works in the structure's frame), stacking floors for belts between levels (a lift or a vertical belt), a bigger bay, power draw by the bay itself, and a shipyard that sells ready-made bays.

## Consequences
- Benefits: constructions are where a player expects them (on the deck, in the module made for it); the build tool is one cursor with no layer to lose; belts can be demolished; the first minutes are about making your own factory; modules differ by what they let you build, which is the point of a modular ship.
- Costs: a floor is a plane of 64 cells per module, so lines are planar and long ones cross modules; layouts that stacked layers (the lab feeds, the ammo battery under a defence module) had to be redrawn on one plane; the first minutes get slower (the workbench is slow by design), which moves the scripted player's win time a little; log version 13 and a rule flag for old logs; the bot and its layouts were rewritten.
- How it is checked: `ConstructionRulesTests` (floor cells and layers, every reject, belts across bays and refused across a hull, vertical belts, turrets on a defence floor, the legacy flag allowing layers and the old placements, the codec version and the old-log flag, hashing of legacy flags, content `buildable` parse and hash, template validation, the starter ship and the workbench recipes), the golden checksum tests on legacy worlds, the structure bot on Relaxed, Normal and Hard (see [structures](../prototypes/structures.md#construction-rules)), and headless screenshots (`--hud-shot`, `--structures-shot`, `--walk-shot`, `--ui-shot`).

## Alternatives considered
- **A. Floor-only building in buildable modules, behind a legacy flag, with a workbench start (chosen).** Smallest change that gives every request: the rule is a placement check, the starter ship loses one module's worth of content and gains nothing new in the simulation (the workbench existed), and old logs replay.
- **B. A new `ModuleKind` for the bay and leave the workshop alone.** Rejected: the enum is part of every module log and of two other designs in flight (the weapons and shipyard work adds kinds too); a second kind that does what the workshop does would make old workshops a dead name. The workshop is renamed in the interface and keeps its id; the owner's "buildable module" is the same module with a clearer name and a price.
- **C. Keep layers and only forbid non-buildable modules.** Rejected: it fixes "which modules" and not "constructions at floor level"; the floating stack of lines in the volume is what the owner objected to.
- **D. Stacked floors with vertical belts (a bay with several decks).** Rejected for now: it keeps all the layer UI and none of the clarity; a taller bay is a later module kind.
- **E. A new `DemolishAt(cell)` command that removes whatever occupies a cell, tag 43.** Rejected: `RemoveSegment` and `RemoveMachine` already do it and the bug was in the picker; a per-cell removal would have to split belt segments, a bigger change than the request.
- **F. Gate the bay behind a research node.** Rejected: it would add a guided step and a lab before the first factory, and the research needs a factory to feed a lab. The price in plates is the gate.
- **G. Remove the workbench and give the starter ship a tiny fixed factory instead.** Rejected: that is the pre-built workshop again under another name; the workbench is a hold-driven crafting table the player runs by hand.
- **H. Make the rule the default and have no flag.** Rejected: ADR 0023 and 0027 set the convention that an old log replays as recorded; a log with machines at layer 2 would be refused by the new check.
