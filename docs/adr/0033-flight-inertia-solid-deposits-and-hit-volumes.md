# 0033. The ship has inertia, deposits are solid, hit volumes follow the drawn models, and engine research raises the speed; log version 15

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-24
- Area: simulation, content, gameplay, client
- Design: [flight feel prototype](../prototypes/flight-feel.md)

## Context
Hand-testing the flight after [ADR 0028](0028-world-generation-obstacles-and-collisions.md), [0029](0029-docking-ship-and-station-are-different.md) and [0030](0030-ship-attitude-in-the-simulation.md) found five faults:

1. A ship flew through the rock of a mineral deposit. Deposits were deliberately not solid in ADR 0028.
2. Approaching an asteroid, part of the ship was drawn inside the rock before the collision fired: the hit volume was the generated radius against the module boxes, and neither the drawn rock nor the drawn hull matches them.
3. Thrust was instantaneous: the velocity went from zero to 5.7 cells a second in one tick, so flying had no weight and a speed readout showed nothing to watch.
4. Nothing in the game made the ship faster or more agile.
5. The view gave little sense of speed (a gizmo and a number, dust streaks of one fixed length that faded in only above 4 cells a second).

The simulation is deterministic and integer only ([ADR 0002](0002-deterministic-simulation-core.md)); every change is a logged command ([ADR 0003](0003-commands-as-the-only-way-to-change-the-world.md)); old command logs must replay as recorded and the golden checksums of the legacy worlds must not move; the scripted player ([structure bot](../prototypes/structure-bot.md)) must still win Relaxed, Normal and Hard at about the same time.

## Decision
- **Flight with inertia.** In a world with structure rules and a `world.flight` content section (`accel_ticks`, `brake_ticks`), a thrust vector or axis intent no longer moves the ship: it sets a *target velocity*, the thrust times the top speed of the engines now (engine percent, boost, cloud slow and power satisfaction as before). The ship's velocity (new player state, thousandths of a unit per tick per axis, with a sub-milli position remainder) approaches the target every tick by at most one step of length `top speed / accel_ticks` (`top / brake_ticks` when the target is shorter than the velocity or points the other way, so turning round brakes first), the step taken along the straight line from velocity to target; the position moves by the velocity. Top speed is the engine percent of [ADR 0006](0006-ship-movement-as-intent-commands-and-ranges.md) of 48 units a tick, so one engine reaches 6 cells a second in 36 ticks (1.2 s) and stops in 18 (0.6 s). Boost raises the target and, because the limits are fractions of the top speed, the acceleration with it. A velocity above the engine speed (a boost that ended, a cloud) is shed at the braking rate or at `speed / (2 * brake_ticks)`, whichever is larger. All integers; the position carries the sub-unit remainder, so nothing is lost. A ship that is docked, charging a jump, lost or being rebuilt has its velocity set to zero (`Player.ClearMotion`); the world edge zeroes the component it clamps. Docking uses the larger of the commanded and the real speed, so a ship still coasting is too fast to dock. Nothing else about commands changes: `SetThrust` and `SetMovement` mean "the speed I ask for", `Docked` and `TooFast` rejects stay.
- **Collisions use the real velocity, and a rock takes the velocity into it.** The closing speed is the real step of the ship (as before, now of a ship with inertia). A solid hit pushes the ship out as before and removes the component of the velocity that points into the rock, so a ship that rams a rock is stopped by it and strikes once, not on every tick of the cooldown.
- **Deposits are solid.** With `world.collision.deposits_solid`, the rock of a revealed, not exhausted deposit is a sphere that ships hit like an asteroid: oriented module boxes against the sphere ([ADR 0030](0030-ship-attitude-in-the-simulation.md)), damage by closing speed above the safe speed and below the cap, the module that hits takes it, push-out. Its centre is the deposit's position, its radius the drawn rock: `DepositRules.NominalRadiusUnits(initial)` times `deposit_hit_percent`. The rock scale (0.6 to 1.6, from the initial amount) is one function in the simulation, `DepositRules.RockScalePermille`, and the game scales the model by it, so drawn and hit size cannot drift apart. The damage is `deposit_damage` (6, a small rock's). A station never moves into a rock, and a docked ship is held by its port, so neither collides; hidden and exhausted deposits are not solid (they are not drawn). A new event field `Deposit` says which one was hit.
- **Mining and building near deposits keep working.** Lasers reach 10 and 14 cells from the module's centre, the rock is under 3.7 cells with the padding, so mining never needs a touch (checked). Drills and drones measure from the deposit's centre as before (a drill reaches 16 cells; a module is 8). A station core or module whose box would reach into a deposit's rock is refused with the new reject `OverlapsDeposit` (founding a station and `PlaceModuleIn` on a station); a ship's own modules are not checked (the push-out handles them).
- **Hit volumes follow the drawn size, plus a hull padding.** `--measure-models` (a game flag) builds every rock, mine, crystal, wreck, debris and deposit model and prints how far its vertices reach in units of the radius the simulation knows. The rocks reach 1.82 times it at most (95 percent of the vertices within 1.53), the debris 1.23, the deposits 1.5 to 1.85 (ice and quartz crystals 2.5 at the tips). Each solid or drifter kind gets `hit_percent` (rocks 155, debris 110), deposits `deposit_hit_percent` 150; and ships add `hull_pad_units` (120, half a cell) to what their modules hit, because the hull is drawn a little beyond its module boxes (the padding also covers the crystal tips). The padding is for ships' modules only: stations, ports and docked ships are not padded, so docking and building are unchanged. Mines, crystals and clouds act by distance as before and wrecks are not solid (a wreck is salvaged by lingering near it), so none of them has a hit volume.
- **Engine research.** Two bonus kinds, `engine_speed` (top speed, past the engine-count cap) and `engine_accel` (acceleration and braking), and three nodes: `core:ion_thrusters` (80 plates, +25 percent speed), `core:thrust_vectoring` (120 plates and 20 carbon, +40 percent acceleration) and, in the engineering field, `core:plasma_drive` (35 engineering science and 200 plates, +35 percent speed and +40 percent acceleration). All three are optional for the win and deliverable by belt like the rest. Shown in the research panel and, as the engine's numbers, on the speed gauge. A Mk2 engine module was not added (see below).
- **Legacy flags and log version 15.** `WorldRules.LegacyCollisions` (flags bit 6, 64) switches off the hit sizes, the padding and solid deposits; `WorldRules.LegacyFlight` (bit 7, 128) switches off inertia and the engine bonuses. Both are off in every preset and in the new-game screen. A log of version 14 or older is read with both on, so it replays as recorded; the new version adds no command. They hash as default rules when they are the only non-default rules. The new player state is mixed only when non-zero and the new content values only when set, so worlds without inertia hash as before, and the shipped legacy content (no `flight` section, no padding) behaves as before whatever the flags say.
- **Client.** The simulation exposes the velocity, the top speed and the acceleration limit (`ShipVelocityMilli`, `ShipTopSpeedMilli`, `ShipAccelMilli`). The game uses them for: the speed readout against the top speed with a throttle gauge (the white tick is the speed the thrust asks for, the fill the real speed); prograde and retrograde rings on the 3D view, computed from the world velocity so they depend on no up; dust streaks that lie along the velocity and stretch with the speed (0.35 to 11 cells long); a field-of-view kick (up to 9 degrees at boost) and an engine rumble that follow speed and acceleration; the engine flame length (idle to boost); and the engine sound's pitch. None of it is saved or hashed.

## Consequences
- Benefits: flying has weight and feedback; a rock is a rock whether it is an asteroid or the ore you mine; the hit volume is checked against the drawn models by a tool, not guessed; old logs, saves and goldens do not move; speed is a thing to research.
- Costs: the scripted player takes about a minute longer (it flies with the ramp and stops short of the rocks); stations need a clear spot next to a deposit (the bot looks for one); a ship cannot touch a deposit, so hand mining is always from a distance (it was already within the laser's range); one reject, one event field, two flags and one log version for every consumer of the log; the content hash changes (old saves refuse to load, like every content change).
- How it is checked: `FlightFeelTests` (ramp, braking, boost, turning round, jump and loss zero the velocity, docking speed, determinism and replay, legacy flight equals content without inertia, an old log replays, flags roundtrip and hash as default rules, solid deposits and their exceptions, mining from the rock's side, station founding and extension, hit radius and padding with and without attitude, engine research), the existing golden checksum tests (the content they strip has no new section), the scripted player winning Relaxed, Normal and Hard, and screenshots of the speed feel and the contact with rocks.
- Not decided here: damage to a rock (deposits do not erode on a hit), a Mk2 engine module (it needs a model; the research nodes cover the progression), a collision shape other than a sphere per rock, an option to reduce the camera effects, a flight assist toggle (a "drift" mode that keeps the velocity while the thrust is zero is the plain behaviour of the thrust being off; it already brakes), the netcode.

## Alternatives considered
- **A. Velocity as player state with an acceleration limit toward a target (chosen).** Smallest change to the commands, one rule for thrust, intent, boost, clouds and power, exact in integers.
- **B. Smooth the client's thrust vector.** Rejected: the simulation would still move at the commanded speed, so collisions, docking speed and the bot would not see the ramp, and the log would stay the same shape for no gain.
- **C. A new throttle command (tags 51 to 55).** Rejected: the thrust vector already is a throttle (its length is the share of the top speed), and the client already sends it at most ten times a second. No new command was needed, so the reserved tags stay unused.
- **D. Solid deposits as obstacles in the galaxy list.** Rejected: deposits are already state with an amount, a reveal flag and a richness; a second record would have to follow all three.
- **E. A bigger hit radius only.** Rejected: the padding of the hull and the size of the rock are different errors (one is about the ship, the other about the model) and the padding must not apply to ports and stations.
- **F. Fix the models to match the radius.** Rejected: the rocks would shrink to about 55 percent of their size and the generated radii, which tune the clearances of the galaxy, would stop meaning what they say; the measured size is now content.
- **G. A Mk2 engine module for the speed progression.** Rejected for now: it needs a model and a place in the designer, which other changes were editing; research nodes give the same numbers through content.
- **H. Keep deposits passable and make the laser the only thing near them.** Rejected: it is the fault that was reported.
