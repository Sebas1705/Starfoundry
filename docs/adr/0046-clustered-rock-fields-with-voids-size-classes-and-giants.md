# 0046. Clustered rock fields: voids, clusters of rocks in size classes with rare giants, and a batched renderer; log version 26

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-27
- Area: simulation, content, client
- Builds on: [ADR 0040](0040-infinite-rock-field-generated-on-demand.md) (the infinite rock field), [ADR 0033](0033-flight-inertia-solid-deposits-and-hit-volumes.md) (solid deposits), [ADR 0036](0036-free-fire-recoil-hold-overflow-and-resource-density.md) (density, mining speed)
- Study: [resource fields](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-27-resource-fields-design.md) (candidate D), measured with [`tools/Starfoundry.FieldStudy`](../../tools/Starfoundry.FieldStudy/Program.cs)

## Context
The owner asked, twice, for much more gap between resources and many more concentrations: "there are too few asteroids and they are too small". The study measured the field of ADR 0040 and agreed: a uniform scatter, about 35 rocks in every scanner sphere, no concentration anywhere, a median rock 1.2 cells in radius (a 5-pixel dot at the edge of the scanner). It also found the rendering cost to fix before clusters of hundreds of rocks: two nodes and two draw calls per rock and one freshly built mesh per slot id.

## Decision
- **Parents per super-sector.** Space is also cut into super-sectors of 800 cells (`clusters.super_cells`). A super-sector holds no parent (30 percent), one (50) or two (20), decided by an integer hash of the world seed and its three coordinates. A parent has a centre, a richness 1 to 5 (weights 35/30/20/10/5), a radius (`80 + 40 * richness` cells, 80 to 120 percent), a shape (50 percent sphere, 30 percent flat belt four times thinner on one axis and one and a half times wider, 20 percent hollow shell) and a dominant ore (the distance table of ADR 0040 at the centre). Its id packs the super-sector and the index, so a rock can name its cluster (`ClusterField.ParentById`).
- **A sector's rocks are the thinned sum of the parents that reach it** (`ClusterField.Generate`, a pure function of the seed, the content, the resource density rule and the sector's coordinates, as in ADR 0040): up to 120 candidates, each kept with the probability `(background + sum of core rate * falloff) / candidates`, with the falloff flat-topped out to 0.58 of the radius (a ring peaking at 0.7 for a shell). The core rate is 24, 32, 44, 56 and 70 rocks per sector for richness 1 to 5 at density 100 (times the resource density rule). Because parents come from super-sector hashes and the sector reads every parent that reaches it, **a cluster that crosses sector faces agrees on both sides** with no state and in any visit order. Strays outside clusters come at 0.25 rocks per sector, so voids are not perfectly empty.
- **Size classes and giants.** Small rocks (mass 300 to 1,200, about 65 percent), medium (1,200 to 4,000, 28 percent) and large (4,000 to 14,000, 4 to 9 percent, more in richer clusters); **giants** (14,000 to 120,000, skewed to the low end) at 0.8 percent of a cluster's rock rate, drawn first so they claim their room. The radius law is the old cube root times **250 percent** (`radius_percent`): the median rock is 3.2 cells in radius, a giant up to 16. The ore is still `mass * density * unit_amount / 100000`; the density of a cluster rock is `50 + 25 * richness` plus or minus 20 (richer clusters mine faster, ADR 0036), strays 40 to 100. 75 percent of a cluster's rocks hold its dominant ore.
- **Margins.** Regular rocks keep 14 cells from the faces of their sector and giants 30, so the drawn rock (1.6 radii) never crosses a face and two rocks of neighbouring sectors never overlap; content validation checks the margins against the biggest class. A sector holds at most 80 rocks.
- **Clearances do not change.** Every candidate keeps the 470-cell exclusion from every system anchor and the world limit; veins and the campaign are untouched.
- **Mining reaches the surface of big rocks.** A ship stops at the hit surface of a solid rock (ADR 0033), which for a giant is 24 cells from its centre, farther than a laser reaches. In the clustered field the laser range and the hand-mining range add the rock's hit radius (`MineReachExtraUnits`); the uniform field keeps the old distance to the centre.
- **Streaming.** Unchanged in its rules (load radius 360, keep radius 480, pins, the table of mined rocks, slot reuse), with two differences in the clustered field: sectors load **nearest first** (a rich cluster can fill the cap, and the sectors that wait must be the far ones), and `max_loaded` is 4,000 (the shipped value; 1,500 before). A sector is never forgotten while a mining order, a drill link, a drone assignment or ore in flight uses one of its rocks.
- **Content.** The optional `world.field.clusters` object holds every number above (all of it validated: margins, shares, weights, ranges). It joins the content hash only when present.
- **Compatibility.** Log version **26**; it adds no command. `WorldRules.LegacyRockClusters` (value 2 of the third flags byte, rules format 3 with `LegacyInterior`) turns the clusters off: the uniform generator of ADR 0040, `max_loaded` aside, runs as before. It is off in every preset and in the new-game screen. Logs of versions 5 to 25 are read with it on, so the logs of versions 21 to 25 replay byte for byte (a test records a flight, rewrites the header to each of 21 to 25 and compares the checksum); it hashes as default rules (the golden checksums do not move). A field world's hash changes as soon as a ship is within the load radius of a sector that holds rocks, as in ADR 0040.
- **Client.**
  - `RockField` draws the streamed rocks with **MultiMesh batches, one per loaded sector**. Near the camera (110 cells from the sector box, 25 of hysteresis) a sector draws its rocks as one MultiMesh per ore, size class, richness bucket and depletion level, each of a shared two-surface mesh (rock with its vein paint, plus the ore chunks); farther, one MultiMesh per size class of a shared 80-face mesh tinted per instance with the ore colour. Meshes are cached per ore, class, bucket, level and variant, not per slot; shapes are shared by every ore of a class, a sector picks its variant, and each instance has its own rotation and a small stretch. The vein width follows the density bucket as before; chunks shrink in three levels as the rock is mined instead of five percent steps. Batches are rebuilt only when a rock of the sector appears, is revealed, changes level or is emptied (at most ten per frame, nearest first), so a depleted rock simply leaves its batch.
  - Rocks stay individually targetable: hovering and picking are rays against the spheres the simulation owns, not against nodes; the target, the laser and the drills use the deposit id as before. `--rock-nodes` keeps the old one-node-per-rock path for comparison.
  - `ClusterGlow` draws a **far impostor** for every cluster within 2,800 cells (a soft glow tinted by the dominant ore, on a shell 470 cells away with the angular size of the cluster, fading as its sectors load), computed from the parent function without any simulation state. It shows where a concentration is before it is scanned: a design choice recorded here; the rocks themselves stay hidden until a scan, as in ADR 0040.
  - Words: a rock that belongs to a cluster says so in its tooltip ("Part of: iron ore cluster (richness 3 of 5)"), a scan that reveals cluster rocks names the cluster, and the size words keep their classes (the scale is divided by the radius law's 250 percent).
- **Not done.** A floating origin (float precision beyond about 50,000 cells, study section 6) and a galactic-plane bias are later work; the world limit of 4,000,000 cells is unchanged.

## Consequences
- Measured, at resource density 100 in a far region of 9,600 sectors (`tools/Starfoundry.FieldStudy -- content/core.json shipped`): @@STATS@@
- Balance: the bots never leave the systems and never mine rocks, and the rocks keep the same clearance. @@BOT@@
- Rendering, measured in the engine at the centre of the biggest cluster nearby with `--field-perf`: @@RENDER@@
- The generator costs about 15 microseconds per sector (the study's prototype cached parents: 4.5); a jump that loads 67 sectors costs about a millisecond.
- Every change of the generator's numbers needs a new log version and the flag, as in ADR 0040.
- Default resource density is 140 percent, so a default world has 40 percent more rocks per cluster than the study's numbers (the study's recommended 1.35 multiplier on the core rate is therefore already in).

## Alternatives considered
- **A. Keep the uniform scatter and make the rocks bigger.** Does not give voids or places; rejected by the study.
- **B. Noise-thinned uniform field (candidate B).** Voids and ridges but no cluster identity, no per-cluster ore and no richness; rejected by the study.
- **C. Cluster parents in a shared world cache.** A cache of parents would be state that must not change a result; the pure function with no cache costs a few microseconds more and keeps order independence obvious.
- **D. Per-rock nodes with a distance cull.** Keeps the cost linear in loaded rocks; the batches make it linear in sectors and ores.
- **E. Godot visibility ranges for the tiers.** They are per node and measured to the node, not to the sector box; the batches switch tiers themselves with a margin.
