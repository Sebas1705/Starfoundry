# 0040. An infinite field of resource asteroids, generated on demand from the world seed; log version 21

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-26
- Area: simulation, content, client
- Builds on: [ADR 0007](0007-galaxy-as-regions-of-one-space-with-deterministic-generation.md) (deterministic galaxy), [ADR 0028](0028-world-generation-obstacles-and-collisions.md) (wide galaxy), [ADR 0036](0036-free-fire-recoil-hold-overflow-and-resource-density.md) (resource density, deposits that regrow)
- Log version 20 and ADR 0039 belong to the blueprint editor (see the [design](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-blueprint-editor-design.md)); this change takes 21 and 0040.

## Context
The galaxy is a finite set of systems (a 9 by 9 grid of cells, 1800 cells apart) and the ship stops at a cube of 10000 cells. Outside the systems space is empty, so exploring runs out. The owner asked for procedural, infinite generation: content produced on demand from the seed, in chunks, with no hard boundary, bounded memory, a deterministic result that does not depend on the order of the visits, asteroids of different size and density, and no change to the pacing of the campaign.

The simulation is deterministic and integer only ([ADR 0002](0002-deterministic-simulation-core.md)); saves are command logs replayed ([ADR 0016](0016-saves-are-the-command-log-replayed.md)). Deposits are the ids that hand mining, drills and drones use, and every client and bot loops over `0 .. DepositCount`.

## Decision
- **Scope of this step: resource asteroids.** The named systems of ADR 0007 and 0028 stay finite (they hold the campaign: jump goals, lanes, stars, hazards and the home start). The infinite part is a **rock field** around and beyond them. Obstacle kinds, hazards and new systems in the field are later work on the same sector scheme.
- **Sectors.** Space is cut into cubes of 200 cells (`sector_cells`). The rocks of a sector are a **pure function of the world seed, the content, the resource density rule and the sector's three coordinates** (`SectorField.Generate`): a SplitMix64 stream seeded by hashing the seed with each coordinate. It does not read the world, the tick or any other sector, so the same sector gives the same rocks in any visit order and at any time. A sector holds 0 to 5 rocks (scaled by the density rule: 140 gives 40 percent more, as for the extra veins of ADR 0036), each kept 8 cells from the sector faces so neighbours never overlap, 3 cells apart from each other in the sector, 470 cells (`exclusion_cells`) from every system anchor and inside the world limit.
- **Size and density decide the amount.** A rock has a **mass** (in thousandths of a reference rock, 300 to 8000, drawn with an exponent of 4 so most rocks are small and a few are huge) and a **density** in percent (40 to 180, the same scale as veins: it also sets the mining speed, ADR 0036). The ore it holds is `mass * density * unit_amount / 100000` (`unit_amount` 60: a mass-1000 rock at density 100 holds 60 ore; the rarest big, dense rock about 860). Its radius is the cube root of the mass times the reference radius (the same rock the collision and the client use): a rock of mass 8000 is twice as wide as one of mass 1000. The rule of the world `DepositRichnessPercent` scales the ore as it does for veins. The item is drawn by weight among those allowed at that distance from home (iron, carbon and ice anywhere; titanium from 2500 cells, nickel from 3500, quartz from 5000).
- **Rocks are deposits.** A loaded rock is an ordinary `Deposit` in the list (`Streamed`, with its own `Radius`), so hand mining, the laser, drills, drones, sensors, the scanner and collisions work unchanged and ids stay `0 .. DepositCount`. Mining, drilling and drone mining consume `Remaining` as always; **when it reaches zero the rock is destroyed**: it is hidden, not solid, and never regrows (the regrowth of ADR 0036 is for veins).
- **Streaming and bounded memory.** Every 15 ticks (`step_ticks`) each ship loads the sectors whose box is within 360 cells (`load_cells`, more than the scanner's 300) and the world forgets loaded sectors farther than 480 cells (`keep_cells`) from every ship. Rocks start hidden; a scan or a sensor reveals them. A forgotten sector frees its slots (`Deposit.Free`, reused lowest slot first), so the list stays near `max_loaded` (1500) rocks however far the ship goes; a load that would pass the cap waits. A sector is **not** forgotten while a mining order, a drill link, a drone assignment or ore in flight uses one of its rocks.
- **State the world must remember.** Only what the player changed: when a sector is forgotten, rocks that lost ore go into a small table `(sector, index) -> ore taken` (an emptied rock stays emptied), and when the sector loads again the same rocks come back with that ore gone. Reveal state is not kept (a forgotten rock must be scanned again). The deposit accounting of the validator counts the table, so `extracted + regrown = hand + drills + drones` still holds.
- **No hard boundary.** With the field the limit of the ship, stations, enemies and projectiles is `world_limit_cells` (4,000,000 cells; positions stay within int range) instead of the 10000-cell cube.
- **Compatibility.**
  - The command log version is 21. It adds no command. `WorldRules.LegacyField` (bit 4 of the second flags byte, off in every preset and in the new-game screen) turns the whole feature off: no field, the old world cube. Logs older than version 21 are read with it on, so they replay as recorded; it hashes as default rules (like the other legacy flags). A world is a field world when the content has a `world.field` object and the flag is off.
  - New state is hashed only when it exists: nothing is mixed until the first rock is loaded; then the serial, the loaded sectors and their slots, the table of forgotten rocks, the free slots and each rock's identity and radius are. The `field` object is part of the content hash only when present, and the golden checksums use content without it.
  - A save is still the command log, so the loaded set and the slot ids follow from the replay; the order of loading is by player id and sector coordinates.
- **Client.** A rock is drawn at the scale the simulation gives (`DepositRockScalePermille`), so size shows; density keeps its tint and chunk count; the target, hover and mining lines show the remaining ore and the size word (small, medium, large, huge) next to the density word. The client rebuilds a rock's model when its slot holds another rock (`DepositSerial`). A floating origin for the renderer (float precision far from the origin) is not done yet.

## Consequences
- Benefits: the universe has no end; nothing about a sector is stored; the campaign (systems, home, pacing) is untouched because the field starts 470 cells from every anchor and the legacy path is a flag; the generator is a small pure function that later kinds of content can reuse.
- Costs: a field world's hash changes as soon as the ship leaves the area around home, so replays and saves of the field depend on the generator: any change of the scheme or its numbers needs a new log version and the flag. Every loop over deposits skips free slots (`Deposit.Free`). Sector and slot ids are internal to a run.
- Balance. About 3.4 rocks per sector at the Normal density and a mean of about 120 ore per rock give roughly 400 ore per sector, about 5000 ore inside one scanner sphere: one system's worth of veins, so the field is a reward for travelling, not a faster start. The scripted player never leaves the systems, and the Normal bot still wins at the same time as before (the table is in the pull request).
- How it is checked: `FieldTests` (order-independent generation, keys, bounds, clearance from systems and between rocks, the home zone, distributions of size and density, amount equals size times density, items by distance, the density rule, cube root, streaming, bounded slots and memory, hidden rocks and the scan, destruction on depletion, memory across forgetting, pins, no world limit and the legacy limit, replay and clone checksums, old logs, rules flag, content validation, loose time budgets), the version asserts of the codec tests, the balance bot with and without `--legacy-field`.

## Alternatives considered
- **A. Make the systems infinite too.** Rejected for this step: systems carry lanes (a connected graph), depth, jump goals and per-system threat state in arrays sized at creation. The sector scheme can later place systems on the same grid; here the campaign stays finite.
- **B. A separate id space for streamed rocks.** Rejected: every client and bot loop uses `0 .. DepositCount`; slots in one list keep them working.
- **C. Keep every generated rock forever.** Simple, but memory grows with the distance flown; the table of changed rocks grows only with what the player mines.
- **D. Hash the full loaded state without a flag and let old logs diverge.** Rejected: old logs must replay byte for byte.
- **E. Generate rocks on the client only.** Rejected: mining and drones are simulation rules.
