# 0035. A personal inventory, one move command for every container, and a fuel slot in each reactor

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-24
- Area: simulation, gameplay, client

## Context
Until now items lived in two places the player could not touch: the ship's hold (one count per item, no slots) and, since [ADR 0029](0029-docking-ship-and-station-are-different.md), a station's stock. Moving between them was `TransferItems`, and everything else happened implicitly: a depot delivers to the hold, a reactor takes hydrogen from the stock and the hold by itself ([ADR 0019](0019-power-and-fuel.md), [ADR 0029](0029-docking-ship-and-station-are-different.md)), a machine is fed by belts only. The owner's pilot feedback (part 2 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-24-pilot-feedback-round-design.md`, section I) asks for a visual, RPG-style inventory so the player can carry items from the ship to the person and from the person into machines, the reactor first.

Constraints that stay: the simulation is deterministic and integer only ([ADR 0002](0002-deterministic-simulation-core.md)); every change is a logged command ([ADR 0003](0003-commands-as-the-only-way-to-change-the-world.md)); old logs replay as recorded and a world that never uses the new state hashes as before ([ADR 0016](0016-saves-are-the-command-log-replayed.md), the legacy-flag idiom of ADR 0023, 0027, 0028, 0031, 0032 and 0033); the scripted player must be unaffected.

## Decision
- **Containers.** `ContainerRef(Kind, Id, Mx, My, Mz)` names six places: `Person` (the commanding player's own bag), `Hold`, `Stock` (a station), `Reactor` (the fuel slot of a reactor module: the structure plus the module coordinate), `MachineIn` and `MachineOut` (the buffers of a machine), and `Void`, the bin, a target that takes everything and gives nothing back. What a slot number means depends on the kind: in the bag it is one of 24 slots (`InventoryRules.Slots`) holding one stack of one item; the hold, a stock and the machine buffers have no slots in the simulation, so their slot number is the item index (as a target, `AnySlot` or the item's own index); a reactor has one slot, number 0.
- **Stacks.** A bag slot holds at most the item's stack size: the content's optional `stack_size` on an item, else `InventoryRules.DefaultStackSize` (100). The shipped content sets 20 for the fuel cell. `stack_size` is mixed into the content hash only when set, so content that sets none hashes as before.
- **`MoveItems(from, fromSlot, to, toSlot, count)`** (tag 56) moves items between any two containers. A count of zero or less means as many as the target takes. A count above what the source slot holds is `NotEnoughItems`; above what the target takes, `NoRoom` (a move is never partial unless the count is zero). A wrong item for a slot is the new reject `WrongItem` (a reactor takes hydrogen or fuel cells and one kind at a time; a machine input takes what its recipe, turret or repairer uses, up to the cap belts fill it to; nothing goes into an output buffer). A bad slot address, the same container twice or a move out of the bin is `InvalidArgument`. Dropping a whole bag stack on a bag slot that holds another item swaps the two; a part of a stack does not (`WrongItem`). Several stacks of the same item merge, and `toSlot = AnySlot` fills the stacks of the item that have room before empty slots.
- **Reach.** Both containers must be usable now: the bag always; the hold while the ship is not lost; a stock, a reactor or a machine of the player's when it belongs to the ship they fly, to the station their ship is docked at, or lies within `InventoryRules.ReachCells` (12) of the ship (`OutOfRange` otherwise, `NotAllowedHere` for another player's, `NoSuchStructure` or `UnknownMachine` for missing ones). Stocks and reactors need the structure rules.
- **`SetFeedSource(structure, mx, my, mz, source)`** (tag 57) chooses where a reactor takes fuel from when its own slot is empty: `Hold` (the default, which is exactly the old behaviour: the station stock of the structure first, then the owner's hold), `None`, `Inventory` (the owner's bag, a fuel cell first) or `HoldThenInventory`. The default is stored as no entry. A reactor always burns its fuel slot first (`StepPower`); the feed source only applies when it is empty. A removed reactor loses its slot and setting, as it loses the burn it was running.
- **`SortInventory`** (tag 58) merges the bag's stacks and packs them to the front in item order, one command instead of a dozen moves.
- **`TransferItems` is a `MoveItems`.** The command keeps its tag, its validation and its rejects; its work is now `MoveCore(hold, stock, ...)` with the clamped count it always had, so its behaviour, accounting counters and every old log are unchanged.
- **Accounting.** A move between the hold and anything else uses `HoldOut` and `HoldIn`, so `HoldIn - HoldOut` still equals what the holds hold. Items put into a machine buffer by hand and taken out of one are counted in `HandIntoMachines` and `HandOutOfMachines`, which join the belt conservation law (`Spawned + MachineOutputsCreated + HandIntoMachines = Consumed + Destroyed + MachineInputsConsumed + InTransit + held + HandOutOfMachines`). Fuel burned from a slot or the bag simply disappears.
- **Hash, copy, validation.** A player's bag is mixed into the checksum only while it holds something; a reactor's slot and a non-default feed source only while they exist; the two hand counters only while non-zero. The clone copies all of it and `ValidateInvariants` checks stacks, slot items, feed values and the counters.
- **Compatibility.** Log version 18 adds the three commands (the reader refuses them in older logs). `WorldRules.LegacyInventory` (a new extra flags byte, bit 0, written only when set, so every other rule set keeps its bytes and the format version 1) is set when a log of version 5 to 15 is read: such a world refuses the three commands (`NotAllowedHere`) and the client says the inventory is not available there. The flag alone never changes the hash (`WithoutLegacyFlags`). Because nothing in an old log can reach the new state and the default feed source is the old rule, an old log replays to the same checksum with or without the flag; the flag exists so a pre-16 world cannot grow state its log format cannot have recorded. `WorldRules.Flags` now takes optional arguments, so the next legacy flag changes one method instead of seven.
- **Client.** `F10` (every letter is taken) or a click on a reactor opens the inventory: a bag of 24 slots (icons, count badges, tooltips with name, stack, fuel time and what the item is used for), filter chips (all, ore, parts, fuel, ammo), a sort button and a bin that asks first, beside the container you are working with (hold, station stock, a reactor with its slot, burn time and auto-feed selector, or the machine you aimed at). Drag moves a whole stack, a right drag the larger half, a shift click or double click moves it to the other panel, Enter picks up and drops from the keyboard. The logic of every drag is in the engine-free `InventoryView` and tested; the panel draws and forwards. Text fitting goes through `UiFit` and the icons through `IconCatalog`.

## Consequences
- Benefits: items can be carried, split, sorted, thrown away and fed to machines by hand; the reactor can be fuelled from the inventory or a slot without changing how any existing world plays; one command and one validation path serve every pair of containers, so a later container (a crate, a drone, a turret's ammo slot) is one case in `Peek`, `RoomFor`, `Take`, `Put` and the reach check.
- Costs: log version 18 and three commands for every consumer of the log; a new content field and a changed content hash for the shipped content; a second item counter pair for machines; a flag byte in the rules block.
- Measured: `docs/prototypes/inventory.md` (the StructureBot on seed 20261121 is unchanged: the bot never moves items and the default feed is the old rule).
- How it is checked: `InventoryTests` (every container pair, capacity and stack rules, strict and clamped counts, split, swap, sort, the bin, reach, the reactor slot and its burn time, the four feed modes, machine buffers and conservation, the codec, replay, the legacy gate, the checksum with an unused or emptied bag), `InventoryViewTests` (categories, drags, quick moves, grid padding, tooltips, texts in both languages, the key rows), the golden checksum tests, `IconCatalogTests`, `RejectTextsTests`. `--inventory-shot=path` renders the panel at 1280x720 and 1920x1080 in both languages with the reactor open.
- Not decided here: a reactor fuel slot for the ship's other fuel users, machine input slots that survive the recipe changing, crates and a ground drop (the bin destroys), picking items out of the world on foot, item weight, and the inventory of a second player.

## Alternatives considered
- **A. One `MoveItems` over six container kinds, slots as item indexes where the container has none (chosen).** One validation path, no new storage in the hold or the stock, the hold and the stock stay the cheap counters they are.
- **B. Give the hold and the stock real slots.** Rejected: it changes capacity accounting for every depot, port and recipe and every old log; the hold's capacity is a count and the economy is balanced against it.
- **C. One command per pair (`BagToHold`, `BagToReactor`, ...).** Rejected: a dozen commands and a dozen rejects for what is one rule.
- **D. The reactor fuel slot as part of the hold or an automatic top-up.** Rejected: the owner asked for a visible slot, a burn time and a choice of source; the default must keep today's behaviour.
- **E. A `SetFeedSource` per structure.** Rejected: a ship with two reactors may want one on the hold and one on the bag, and the module coordinate is already how burn state is keyed.
- **F. Move the bag into the checksum always.** Rejected: it would change every golden checksum for a feature old worlds cannot use.
