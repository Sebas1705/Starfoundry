# 0029. Docking: the ship moves, the station is fixed, and the ship docks at its ports

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Area: simulation, gameplay, client

## Context
[ADR 0018](0018-modular-structures.md) made ships and stations one thing, a structure of modules, and left docking, several ships and ship-to-ship transfer out. In practice the two had stayed almost the same: a station was only a structure without a player, nothing stopped the module menu from giving it a cockpit or engines, and the two never met: the ship's hold, the station's machines and the player's walking all lived in separate worlds. The owner wants them to be different things. The ship is mobile, the station is fixed, and the ship docks at the station.

Constraints from earlier decisions: the simulation is deterministic and integer only ([ADR 0002](0002-deterministic-simulation-core.md)); every change goes through a logged command ([ADR 0003](0003-commands-as-the-only-way-to-change-the-world.md)); old command logs replay as recorded and the golden checksums of default and legacy worlds do not move; the hold belongs to the player, not to a structure ([ADR 0019](0019-power-and-fuel.md), [ADR 0022](0022-structure-economy-fixes.md)).

## Decision
- **Fixed station, mobile ship.** A station has no player and no thrust, so its origin only ever changes where the world is built. New rules say it plainly: a station takes no `cockpit` or `engine` module (`KindNotAllowed`) and a ship takes no `core`. Structures are still axis-aligned and unrotated, so docking is a translation.
- **Ports.** A port is a free horizontal face (`PosX`, `NegX`, `PosZ`, `NegZ`) of a module that bears ports: a station's `core`, a ship's `cockpit` (its two flanks only: its nose and its back are not ports) and the new `dock` module on either kind (all four faces). A face is free when no module of the structure touches it and, on a station, no ship is docked there. The new `dock` module costs 10 iron plates and 8 iron ingots and adds 40 to the station stock; it is optional content (older content files load without it).
- **Commands.** `DockAt(station, mx, my, mz, face)`, `Undock` and `TransferItems(item, count, toStation)`: tags 39 to 41, command log version 11. The reader refuses them in logs of versions 5 to 10. New rejects: `Docked`, `NotDocked`, `NoFreePort`, `TooFast`, `NoRoom`, `AlreadyDocked`.
- **Docking rule.** The ship's port is the nearest free port on the face opposite the station's. Docking needs the centre of the two ports within `DockRules.RangeCells` (12) of each other, a commanded speed of at most `DockRules.MaxSpeedUnitsPerTick` (12 units a tick, a quarter of the base speed of 48; computed by integer arithmetic from the thrust vector or axis intent, engines and power), the ship's modules not to intersect any module of another structure at the docked pose, and the station to be the player's. The ship then snaps to the pose that puts its port module on the module cell beyond the station's port (`DockPose`), the pilot goes with it, thrust and intent are cleared, and the ship keeps its structure id.
- **Docked ship.** It cannot thrust or jump (`Docked`; stopping is always allowed), `CanFly` is false, `ShipSpeedUnitsPerTick` is 0 and `MoveShipStructure` does nothing. It leaves the port on `Undock`, which keeps its pose and returns the thrust, or by itself when the station or either port module is destroyed. Players cannot remove a port module in use (`ModuleOccupied`) or build into the space of a docked ship (`Overlap`).
- **The station stock.** Each station keeps a stock of items (`Structure.Stock`, allocated on first use). Its capacity is 60 for the core, 40 per dock module and the hold capacity of each cargo module. `TransferItems` moves items between the docked ship's hold and the stock, as many as fit (a count of zero or less means all); the hold accounting counters are kept (`HoldOut` on unload, `HoldIn` on load).
- **What docking gives.**
  - Fuel: a station's reactors and the reactors of a ship docked at it burn the station stock first (a fuel cell, else hydrogen) and the owner's hold only when the stock has none.
  - Repair: a docked ship regains module hit points three times as fast (`DockRules.RepairSpeedup`) and does so under the power of the station, so a ship with a dead reactor still mends at a powered station.
  - Defence: shield absorption of a docked ship is the better of its own and its station's, and its turrets get the station's defence-module bonus (cap unchanged, 50 percent).
- **Walking across (client).** The body keeps the ship's local frame; while docked the walkable volumes are the ship's modules plus the station's, shifted by the difference of the two origins (a whole number of cells, because docking is lattice aligned). The two modules at the port draw doorways and an airlock; crossing plays the airlock sound and says where you are.
- **Compatibility.** Everything here acts on state that older logs never create (a docked ship, a stock), so no old replay changes: the golden checksums of the legacy and structure worlds are unchanged (the stripped content also drops the `dock` entry), and the hash mixes docking state only while a ship is docked and a stock only while it holds items. The clone, the validator (`ValidateDocking`: docked at an existing station, ports that face each other, ship at the port pose, non-negative stock) and the replay cover the new state.
- **Not changed.** Depots still deliver to the owner's hold, ports still unload from it, and the hold is still global: the stock is a second place to keep items, a refuelling depot and the way a ship leaves cargo behind, not a second economy. The scripted player never docks, so its win times do not move.

## Consequences
- Benefits: ship and station are different things in the rules, the model and the controls; docking has numbers a player can play with (range, speed, ports, stock); the interiors join, so the base is walkable end to end; refuelling and repairing at home are worth the trip; nothing in old logs or saves changes.
- Costs: log version 11 and three commands for every consumer of the log; a new content module kind (the content hash changes, so old saves refuse to load, like every content change); a second set of walkable volumes in the client.
- Measured: `docs/prototypes/docking.md`.
- How it is checked: `DockingTests` (ports, geometry, dock and undock, range, speed, free port, overlap, locked flight, transfers and capacity, fuel from the stock, faster repair, shield and defence cover, destruction of the station or the port, builds refused where a ship sits, replay, clone, checksum, codec and log version, content without the dock module, key panel rows and strings, tip), the golden checksum tests, `AudioTests` for the three new sounds, `RejectTextsTests` for the new rejects. `--dock-shot=path` renders the approach, the docked ship from the cockpit and from outside, and the walk across the airlock.
- Not decided here: several ships docked at one station by choice of the player (the rules allow one ship per port and several ports), ship-to-ship docking, stations that make power for a docked ship's machines, depots that feed the stock, ports that feed from the stock, a docking fee or a cargo exchange with traders.

## Alternatives considered
- **A. Docking as a translation of the ship, ports on core, cockpit and a new dock module (chosen).** Minimal, deterministic, no rotation, reuses the module grid and the structure-local frames, so the interiors join without a new coordinate system.
- **B. A free-form docking port with an orientation.** Rejected: structures have no rotation, so a free orientation would need one, and a lattice aligned pose is what lets the client walk from one local frame into the other.
- **C. Ships always dock with a dedicated dock module.** Rejected: the starter ship would need one before the campaign flow (found a station, dock, unload) works, which moves the first minutes of the game and the scripted player's win times. The cockpit's flanks and the station core are ports, and the dock module only adds ports and storage.
- **D. Depots and ports on a station use the station stock.** Rejected for now: it would split the economy in two and change the balance of every scripted run; the stock is deliberately an addition.
- **E. A new rule flag.** Rejected: nothing needs it, because the new rules only act on a docked ship or a stocked station, which old worlds never have.
