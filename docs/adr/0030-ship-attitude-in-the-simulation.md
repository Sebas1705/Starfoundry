# 0030. Ship attitude in the simulation: a fixed-point quaternion set by a command, oriented hit volumes and ports; log version 12

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Area: simulation, client

## Context
Free flight ([prototype](../prototypes/free-flight.md)) gave the ship a full 6-DOF attitude, but only in the client: a quaternion turned the ship's picture and the simulation kept treating the structure as axis-aligned. A ship rolled 90 degrees therefore still collided, mined, docked and ranged as its upright box (8 by 4 by 40 cells lying along X). The owner asked for collisions that respect the orientation.

Constraints from earlier decisions: the simulation is deterministic and integer only ([ADR 0002](0002-deterministic-simulation-core.md)); every change goes through a logged command ([ADR 0003](0003-commands-as-the-only-way-to-change-the-world.md)); old command logs replay as recorded and the golden checksums do not move ([ADR 0028](0028-world-generation-obstacles-and-collisions.md), [ADR 0029](0029-docking-ship-and-station-are-different.md)); the scripted player never turns, so its win times must not move.

## Decision
- **The attitude is state of a ship structure.** `Structure.Attitude` is a unit quaternion in fixed point (`AttitudeRules.Scale` = 16384 is 1.0), in the canonical sign (`W >= 0`). The identity is the default and is what every structure, every station and every old save has. All the maths (`Attitude`) is `long` arithmetic with a rounding right shift, so every machine computes the same cells; there is no float in the simulation.
- **A new command `SetAttitude(W, X, Y, Z)`** (tag 42, four signed 16 bit fields) sets the attitude of the commanding player's ship. The command layer validates it: structure rules on, a ship, not lost, every component within 1.0, squared length equal to the scale squared within `NormTolerance` (a relative 2.4e-4, about twice the error of rounding a unit quaternion), and a non-identity turn is refused while docked (`Docked`). A quaternion and its negative are one turn and are stored in the canonical sign. Replays and saves reproduce the attitude from the command log, like everything else.
- **What the client sends.** The client keeps its float quaternion for the feel of the mouse and quantises it (`ShipAttitude.Quantize`). `AttitudeThrottle` sends a change of at least a quarter of a degree at most every 0.1 s and any smaller change after 0.3 s, so the simulation always ends with the exact attitude the pilot stopped at, and a pilot who turns for an hour adds about ten commands a second of 13 bytes at the worst (a save replays them; the thrust throttle of [ADR 0027](0027-mining-laser-rules.md) works the same way). A loaded game adopts the simulation's attitude instead of starting level. While a turn is under way the simulation lags the picture by up to 0.1 s.
- **The frame.** A structure turns about the pilot seat: the seat's eye of its first cockpit module (the module (0,0,0) without one), `AttitudeRules.PivotX/Y/Z` from the module origin (4, 2.7 and 6.6 cells, the numbers of the client's `SeatLocator`, checked by a test). `StructureToWorld` is `origin + pivot + R * (point - pivot)` and `WorldToStructure` its inverse. With the identity it is exactly `origin + point`, the old formula, and the identity path skips the maths altogether.
- **Oriented hit volumes.** A module is still a box in the structure's own grid. A sphere (rock, mine, cloud, wreck range, blast) is moved into the structure's frame, clamped to the box there (so the distance is exact and the box is axis aligned whatever the attitude) and the contact normal is turned back into the world. The push-out, the closing speed, the deepest-module rule, the safe speed, the damage formula, the cooldown and the shields of [ADR 0028](0028-world-generation-obstacles-and-collisions.md) are unchanged and use the module actually hit under rotation.
- **Everything that reads a module position goes through the same frame:** `ModuleWorldCentre` (enemy targeting, the structure defence and shield ranges, sensors, the wave forecast), `WorldCentre` of a machine cell (turrets, drills, repairers), the drone bay range, the docking ports (`PortWorldPoint`), the founding overlap test (the axis-aligned bound of the turned modules, which is conservative) and the dock overlap test. Not changed on purpose: the ship's own reference point, the player position, which is the structure origin, stays the point the laser, the scanner, the ship weapon and the build range measure from, so turning the ship in place does not drop a mining order; the origin does not move when the ship turns.
- **Docking alignment.** A port is axis aligned, so docking needs the ship's attitude within about 15 degrees of the identity (`W >= 16244`, `DockRules` via `Attitude.IsDockAligned`): new reject `Misaligned` and `DockCandidate.Aligned`. The ports and the range are measured from the turned ship. On docking the ship snaps to the lattice pose with the identity attitude. The client's assisted approach turns the ship onto the port's axes as it closes in.
- **Compatibility.**
  - The command log version is 12. `SetAttitude` cannot appear in logs of versions 5 to 11 (the reader refuses it, also inside a batch).
  - There is no rule flag. Unlike [ADR 0028](0028-world-generation-obstacles-and-collisions.md), which changed what an old command does, nothing here does: old logs have no `SetAttitude`, so every ship stays at the identity and the identity path computes exactly the old numbers. A flag would only be a switch nothing flips. The `WorldRules` flags byte keeps its free bit 4.
  - The hash mixes the attitude only when it is not the identity (after the other structure state, before the docking state), so the golden checksums hold and a ship that was turned and turned back hashes as one that never was. The clone, the validator (a canonical unit quaternion; only ships; a docked ship is level) and the replay cover it.
- **Not decided here:** ship against station and ship against ship collision (there is none; a docked ship overlaps nothing by construction), docking with a turned port (yawed by a quarter turn, upside down), angular momentum or a turn rate limit in the simulation (the attitude may jump between two commands; the next collision pass pushes the ship out), a pivot that does not jump when the cockpit is destroyed (the pivot follows the first cockpit, so losing it moves a turned ship by the difference of the two pivots).

## Consequences
- Benefits: what you see is what collides: a ship rolled 90 degrees hits where its turned box is, mines and clouds reach the modules where they are, ports and sensors turn with the ship, and docking has a sensible alignment rule; nothing in old logs, saves or goldens moves.
- Costs: one command, one tag and a structure field for every consumer of the log; a 13 byte command about ten times a second while the pilot turns; an extra frame transform on a few call sites; the picture and the simulation differ by up to 0.1 s of turning.
- How it is checked: `OrientedCollisionTests` (the fixed-point matrix against the float quaternion over 2000 random turns, quantising over 50 000, refusal of non-unit quaternions, the pivot equals the client's seat, the command and its refusals, codec round trip and the old-log refusal, an old log replaying identically, checksum unchanged for the identity and restored after a turn back, clone and replay, a rolled ship hitting where its turned box is and passing where the upright box was, the level path unchanged, clouds, module centres, ports and bounds, docking alignment and the level settle, the throttle), and the whole existing suite and the golden checksums unchanged.

## Alternatives considered
- **A. A command carrying a fixed-point quaternion (chosen).** One logged value, no float, any attitude, exact replay.
- **B. Small-angle or Euler components.** Rejected: pitch, yaw and roll compose badly past the vertical (gimbal lock), and the client's attitude is a quaternion that never locks.
- **C. Four axis-aligned orientations only (a quarter turn at a time).** Rejected: the pilot flies at any attitude; snapping the hit boxes to quarter turns would make collisions lie by up to 45 degrees.
- **D. Floating-point attitude in the simulation.** Rejected by [ADR 0002](0002-deterministic-simulation-core.md): two machines may differ in the last bit, and a replay would drift.
- **E. Send the angular velocity and integrate in the simulation.** Rejected for now: it would make the simulation own the turn rate, thrust and torque, and every steering input a command at tick rate; the pilot's attitude is an input, like the thrust vector.
- **F. A rules flag `LegacyAxisAligned`, as `LegacyGalaxy`.** Rejected: an old log never turns the ship, so the flag would change nothing (see Compatibility).
- **G. Pivot about the structure origin.** Rejected: the origin is a corner of the first module; the camera at the seat would swing by up to the ship's length when rolling. The seat is where the pilot is.
